# CANON — TIMELINE
## Historical bedrock of the Cubeo Chronicles

> What Follett does with his archives. Nothing here is invented: it's all drawn from Galnet and
> the Codex. Fiction slips in **between** these dates, never against them.

---

## THE DATE

**9 August 3308.** It's the day of her accident, and canon wrote it for you.

That day, in the **HIP 22460** system, Salvation's superweapon — the **Proteus Wave** — is
activated. The pulse successfully disables every Thargoid in the system. Then a **second pulse,
of unknown origin**, drains all power from the megaship Bright Sentinel. The Thargoid vessels
reanimate. All communications go down. The Alliance, the Federation and the Empire all confirm
they're receiving no signal from any of their capital ships.

The next day, the verdict lands: Thargoid forces have overwhelmed the coalition fleet. A witness
reports that every human vessel in the area went inert, drifting, while Thargoids swarmed
everywhere, in numbers never seen before.

**It's all there.** A mission sold as winnable. Independent pilots explicitly called in as
reinforcements. Ships that simply stop working. A massacre. And no one to tell apart, among the
wrecks, the one whose armour had been falsified from the ones the second pulse alone was enough to
kill.

**It's the perfect crime.** She died among thousands of others. Except she was sent there on
purpose.

And the arithmetic lines up exactly: August 3308 → 3312 = **four years**.

## DECEMBER 3306 — THE GREAT SOLITUDE

**"Support Sirius Atmospherics initiative by providing ammonia world exploration data."**

Dr **Maximilian North**, of Sirius Atmospherics, announces the second phase of the allied
**Coalsack** colonisation plan and puts out an open call: he needs detailed scans of **ammonia
worlds**, to identify the best terraforming candidates under newly developed procedures. Data is
to be turned in at **Betancourt Base**, in **Musca Dark Region PJ-P b6-1** — Coriolis station, 172
Ls from the star, **520 light-years from Sol**.

Campaign end: **10 December 3306**. The top 75% receive a **Detailed Surface Scanner modified to
double the standard probe radius**.

Ammonia worlds are rare. The campaign was notoriously slow, nearly stalled: three thousand five
hundred contributors, and a gauge that refused to move.

**What this gives the story:**

- Five hundred and twenty light-years. The solitude isn't a metaphor here — it's a distance.
- She spent weeks hunting the rarest planets in the galaxy to mark the ones where humans might
  one day **build homes**. A woman who runs from attachment, put to work cataloguing places where
  other people would settle.
- This is the exact window when the doctor got too close. She left.
- **Twenty months before the accident.** The DSS is legitimately hers. It leaves only **one
  anomaly** in her inventory — and it stands out all the more clearly for it.

**The canonical bridge:** Commodore Morag Halloran will later state that her experience **in the
Coalsack** made her the ideal candidate for Salvation. If the Viscountess spent months in the
Coalsack in 3306, the two of them shared the same theatre of operations. That's where an
independent pilot becomes a name on a list.

**Usable names:** Dr Maximilian North (Sirius Atmospherics) · Rodion Stathos, chief xenobiologist
at Betancourt Base.

## BEFORE — 3307

- **Nov 3307** — Aegis, under Admiral **Aden Tanner**, attacks Hind Mine, Azimuth's headquarters.
  Tanner suspects Salvation's work is deliberately provoking the Thargoids.
- **Dec 3307** — **Commodore Morag Halloran**, a senior Alliance Defence Force officer, resigns and
  joins Salvation with nearly a hundred crew. Hundreds of naval officers follow her lead.
- The NMLA, a terrorist movement, is active. **Theta Seven** hides among the **Far God cult**, in
  Mudhrid.

**Use for the chronicles:** this is the era when an independent pilot with a moral code becomes
*useful* to certain people — and then *inconvenient*.

## 3308 — THE YEAR OF THE ACCIDENT

- **05 Jan** — Admiral Tanner's court-martial is suspended after **Ram Tah**'s testimony for the
  defence. He'd been charged with mutiny for attempting to breach Hind Mine.
- **May** — Thargoid invasions of **Didio, Novas and Sosong** are fully repelled thanks to
  Salvation's superweapons. This success is what convinces the Alliance, Empire and Federation to
  officially back Azimuth Biotech.
- **Mid-3308** — Taurus Mining Ventures officially rebrands as **Azimuth Biotech**. Salvation is
  revealed to be **Dr Caleb Wycherley**.
- **08 Jul** — Thargoids invade **HIP 22460**. Halloran calls in every available independent pilot
  and AX squadron, asking them to protect convoys delivering Guardian artefacts to the Bright
  Sentinel.
- **02 Aug** — The superpowers commit: an Alliance megaship, three Farragut-class cruisers and
  **three Majestic-class interdictors** deployed to HIP 22460.
- **08 Aug** — Azimuth announces the Proteus Wave's core mechanism is fully active and a final
  round of safety checks is underway.
- **09 Aug** — **Contact lost.** (see "The Date")
- **10 Aug** — The Thargoids take HIP 22460.
- **25 Aug** — Independent salvage teams confirm Salvation's death, aboard the Bright Sentinel.
- **Sep–Oct** — Eight faster-than-light anomalies are tracked, named *Rogue Signal Sources*.
- **04 Nov** — They converge on the core worlds.
- **17 Nov** — Mission Xeno-Peace: the megaship **Kingfisher** attempts contact with **Taranis**.
  It fails; the Kingfisher is destroyed.
- **08 Dec** — **Cocijo** and **Oya** arrive in turn. The Maelstroms immediately unleash entire
  fleets on neighbouring habitats.

**She saw none of it.**

## 3312 — WHERE AISLING STANDS NOW

She left an abolitionist crusade. She's coming back to an empire.

Under Powerplay 2.0, Aisling Duval closed out its first full year as the **largest Power in the
galaxy** — 2,242 systems, having overtaken Edmund Mahon. And she holds that ground bound into
**ZYADA**, a standing bloc with four other Powers: **Z**emina Torval, **Y**uri Grom, **A**rissa
Lavigny-Duval, **D**enton Patreus, **A**isling Duval.

Two of those names are already on the suspect list (see below). **Zemina Torval** — the slaver
senator, the ideological opposite of everything Aisling claims to stand for. **Denton Patreus** —
the militarist, the alternate lead on who'd falsify a hull's rating and call it reinforced. If
she re-swears an oath she doesn't remember taking, she's swearing it to a coalition that now,
formally, includes both of them. Aisling doesn't have to have known anything. The alliance would
exist with or without her intentions, and that's precisely what makes it useful and deniable in
the same breath.

**What this gives Chapter V and VI:** a political landscape with real teeth, no invention
required. The five allegiance missions can read as pure ceremony on the surface — and, underneath,
as a woman signing herself back into arm's reach of people her own file should be warning her
about, if her file still worked.

## SEPTEMBER 3312 — GALNET, LIVE

Three real Galnet pieces surfaced this session, all usable as background texture or conversation
fodder without inventing anything:

- **03 Sep 3312 — "Wreaken Calls for Surface Mining Support Tests."** Wreaken Corporation
  (Dr Ben Curtis, Chief Geologist) calls independent pilots to Ega/Metz Enterprise to field-test its
  new deployable mining rig and the **Rhino** SRV, developed with Vodel. This is the article behind
  the **already-closed CG** noted in `In-Game Operations.md` §4 (CGID 856, "Wreaken tests its new
  mining rig," expired 10 Sept, Tier 5/50M CR reached) — a concrete in-world source for a CG the
  Viscountess never directly narrates, since it closed before she reached Ega.
- **11 Sep 3312 — "Federation Condemns Ongoing October Accords Exclusion."** President **Felicia
  Winters** accuses the October Accords coalition of weaponising **Radicoida unica** and warns that
  any aggression toward the Federation will be met in kind. Live political backdrop for the trader
  crowd at Metz Enterprise (Ch. II Day 5) — some of them furious on Winters's behalf, none of it
  requiring the Viscountess to declare where she stands.
- **14 Sep 3312 — "Increased Traffic Near Prison Ships Sparks Concern."** Known pirate-faction
  affiliates spotted shadowing prison-transport movements near several reformatories, no
  engagement yet, just sustained observation. No direct link to the Viscountess's own week — usable
  later as atmosphere (something watching, patiently, for reasons not yet given) if the story wants
  it.

## THE ANOMALY — THE MINING LASER

**16 to 23 November 3309 — "Mine Resources for Torval Mining Ltd."**

Curbeam Hub, **LHS 3872** system, Coriolis station, 609 Ls. Campaign run by the **Aristocrats of
Cuacocha** to celebrate **Constantia Torval**'s ascension to the head of Torval Mining Ltd.
Delivery of lithium hydroxide, painite, praseodymium and tritium. Top 75% receive a **mining laser
pre-engineered to Long Range**.

**Fifteen months after her presumed death.**

The module is still sitting in storage at Curbeam Hub. Never fitted. Never claimed. And it's
**class 1** — a hardpoint neither of her Clippers can mount. It could only have been earned with
the Asp.

Someone took her Asp out in November 3309, flew it under her name, mined for a week straight, and
never came back to collect the reward.

**In service to the Torvals.** The slaver family. The exact opposite of everything she'd aligned
herself with behind Aisling Duval.

It's the first hard clue in the chronicles. Canonical, dated, named, and sitting in a hangar 65
light-years from Sol.

## NOTE — THE DSS IS NOT AN ANOMALY

The DSS mounted on the Asp is a standard *Expanded Probe Scanning Radius* grade 5 blueprint —
engineered by her, with no "pre-engineered" label. So it isn't the Betancourt Base reward, which
doubled probe radius. Two modules, two histories, one memory: her mind fuses a true memory onto
the wrong object. That's exactly what the narrator does, and it should stay that way.

For the record, a more recent CG also handed out a pre-engineered DSS, outside her timeline:

**8 September 3311 — "Rackham HIP 87621 Planetary Exploration Initiative."**

Sign-up at **Curie Gateway**, in **Hill Pa Hsi**. Scanning rocky and metal-rich worlds, data
turned in to Universal Cartographics; detailed scans and first discoveries heavily boost
contribution value. Two-week campaign. Pilots in the **top 75% and above** receive a **Detailed
Surface Scanner V1, pre-engineered with extended probe range**.

A second one existed in February 3311 (Trailblazers), at +40% probe range. Neither one is hers.

**Three readings of the mining laser anomaly**, none to be settled before Chapter VI:

1. **Someone took her Asp out.** Maintained, the mechanic says. Taken out, registered, flown,
   brought back. Under her name. Why keep a dead woman's identity active?
2. **Someone was keeping her licence current** — a ship and an identity that stay active aren't
   deregistered. Useful to whom?
3. **The coma didn't run four unbroken years.** There are weeks no one talks about.

**A fourth angle, for the doctor:** she's a seasoned pilot. She'd have had the means. What if it
was her who took the Asp out — to keep the ship alive, the way she kept the body alive?

## A DOOR LEFT AJAR — THE UNCANNY

*(proposal, not policy — nothing here is adopted until it earns its way into the logbook)*

The pitch: something that shakes the world the way the Aes Sedai do — real consequence, real
danger, real awe — without ever being a fairy or a monster. Elite Dangerous already has the
material for this; none of it needs inventing, only leaning on.

**Three anchors already sitting in canon, any of which could host it:**

- **Raxxla.** The oldest unresolved mystery in the setting — a legend passed spacer to spacer for
  centuries, a ghost planet beyond witchspace. Its only textual root, *The Dark Wheel*, describes
  an alien construct on it called the **Omphalos Rift**: a gateway, not to elsewhere, but to other
  universes entirely. Frontier has never confirmed or denied it exists in-game. That silence is
  usable — a rumour a character can half-believe without the story ever having to cash it in.
- **The Guardians.** A precursor civilisation, extinct a million-plus years, wiped out by their
  own **Constructs** — the machines they built to serve them. Their ruins bleed **meta-alloys**,
  the same substance now sold openly at markets. A technology genuinely older than language,
  handled daily by pilots who never ask what it used to be for. That gap between mundane use and
  incomprehensible origin is exactly the Aes-Sedai register: power so normalised nobody's afraid
  of it anymore, except the people who understand it best.
- **The Far God cult / Thargoid caustics.** Already in this timeline (see "Before — 3307" above):
  a cult hiding inside a hostage megaship, a siege that ends in enzyme bombs and mass death. The
  Thargoids' biology — caustic, corrosive, faintly organic in a way human engineering never is —
  is already coded in this story's own recurring motif (**the acid**, see `Guidelines.md`). A
  cult built around something inhuman that answers prayers on its own schedule, never on request,
  would cost nothing new to introduce; it's an intensification of a thread already running.

**A fourth option, closer to home:** the Viscountess reads people by sensation before
understanding — already established, already unexplained. Nothing requires that gift to ever be
named. But if it ever needs a shape, Guardian neuro-tech encountered during her lost Coalsack
months, or the same Roskam facility that may have rebuilt the mechanic, are both already on the
board. No new door would need to be opened — only a light shone into one that's already ajar.

**How to use this, if at all:** the same rule as Raxxla itself — never confirm, never deny, let it
recur just often enough that the reader starts keeping their own list. The moment it's explained
outright, it stops being uncanny and becomes a mechanic. Follett would cut it. Jordan would let it
breathe for four hundred pages first. Do the second.

## GOLDEN RULE

We **borrow** the dates, the places, the public names and the vocabulary. We **invent** the
private people: the mechanic, the doctor, the supplier, the ducal contact. The fiction lives in
the gaps of a true timeline — that's what makes it credible, and it's exactly Follett's method.

Never put words in a canonical character's mouth that they never said. Having them pass through a
scene, however, is fair game.
